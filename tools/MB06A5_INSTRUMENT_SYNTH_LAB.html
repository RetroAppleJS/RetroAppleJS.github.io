What to look for in Audacity

  1. Pitch: Select a steady C4 interval and measure its principal frequency with Analyze → Plot Spectrum. For modulated or detuned sounds there may be multiple peaks; do not treat the highest peak as necessarily the note pitch.
  2. Shape: Zoom around 0.30–0.32 s of each probe. The software DAC saw/triangle should be stepped. A plucked square remains rectangular at its core; its envelope changes amplitude, not the oscillator shape.
  3. Amplitude: Compare RMS/peak levels in the exported JSON. No automatic loudness normalization is applied.
  4. CPU feasibility: Look at the register-write rates and costs before proposing any waveform for the 6502 IRQ player.
  5. Real-emulator repeat: For candidates worth pursuing, make a native E1 loader and record its real WAV through the Mockingboard peripheral. Only that establishes the full timing cost and waveform under HGR load.
The AY cannot generate native triangle or sawtooth tone oscillators. Software-DAC waves use rapid R8 volume updates and are not suitable for the existing MB-06A.3 1,024-cycle scheduler as-is. The buzzer candidate may change perceived pitch or create sidebands; it is an exploratory effect rather than a guaranteed pitch-perfect oscillator.

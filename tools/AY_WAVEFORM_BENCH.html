Comparison includes native square, envelope triangles and software-volume triangles. Automatic selection is used in the frequency survey.

The broadband FFT uses 2,048 samples (10.77 Hz bins). Harmonic comparisons use up to 8,192 centered samples and the actual programmed frequency, so pitch error is separated from spectral-shape error. THD and ideal scores are N/A for modulated, detuned, mixed-noise, or multi-chip sounds.

Interpretation of spectral matching

Pitch-matched: ideal square, sawtooth and triangle spectra are evaluated at each AY fixture's programmed fundamental, not the requested note. The curve comparison therefore isolates timbre from tuning. Saw-up and saw-down have the same ideal magnitude spectrum; direction/phase is not scored.

Metrics: the harmonic shape error is a weighted RMS difference of normalized harmonic levels, in dB, over up to 32 harmonics below Nyquist. Shape score = 100 × exp[−½(error / 12 dB)²]. Cosine match measures linear harmonic-spectrum similarity. Scores are analytic heuristics, not SID fidelity, perceptual quality, or a calibrated correlation.

Triangle reconstruction: Compare hardware envelope shape $A with $E, software 32-step raw register levels, and the calibrated 32-step inverse-DAC reconstruction. The latter renders a 220 Hz reference square tone at each AY volume level, measures RMS, and chooses register levels closest to a linear triangular target. This is a measured emulator response, not a physical AY calibration.

Composite fixtures: Buzzer, noise, tone+noise, detune and two-chip have broadband fingerprints (spectral flatness, rolloff, high-band energy and strongest FFT peaks), but no invalid single-oscillator THD or ideal shape scores. Buzzer envelope retrigger phase can be offset against the running tone; the relative frequency drift is estimated from register periods.

6502 feasibility: 32 register writes/cycle means 1,760 writes/s at 55 Hz or 14,080 writes/s at 440 Hz, excluding VIA overhead, note handling and other game logic. These are experimental comparison fixtures, not an efficient drop-in Apple II implementation. No CPU-cycle feasibility has been demonstrated.

Technical interpretation

Audio-rate envelope limitation: At a 1,021,800 Hz clock, a repeating saw can reach about 3,991.4 Hz and a full triangle about 1,995.7 Hz at envelope period 1. A higher requested pitch is clamped, not achieved. AY envelope shapes are 16-level stepped signals, affected by the real AY DAC curve.

Two-chip experiment: AY0 channel A is rendered on the left and AY1 channel A on the right, like the production Mockingboard; the plotted mono waveform averages those stereo outputs. Each AY has one shared envelope and one shared noise source.

Boundaries: This bench renders audio using the existing RetroAppleJS AYCore. It does not replace its DAC or waveform synthesis, perform a SID comparison, or measure physical hardware. Phase 1.3 compares native and software-generated AY spectra against analytic, pitch-matched ideal references; this is not a physical SID reference recording.
